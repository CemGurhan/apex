- Market makers will place a trade to buy slightly below the fair price to buy on the book or place a trade to sell
slightly above the fair price to sell on the book.

- The profit off of the difference between these trades. E.g if they place a bid at 99.5 and place an ask
at 100.5, they can make a 1 dollar profit. Trying to go wider than that might result in no fils, e.g. why buy
from someone selling at 110 when you can buy at lower.

- The reason they do this is because they do not have to make a directional bet. E.g. they could try and predict that
they'd be long if they buy at 90 and market moves eventually to 120. But it might not. With market making, they're allowing the
market to come to them instead of them going to the market. 

- The orders on either side will be placed simultaneously. If the ask is bought first, they're short x units. Eventually,
the sale to the buy order will cover the units owed from the sale, any remaining is profit.

- The converse happens with your bid getting filled first. You're now long x units and you're hoping someone
will buy your ask order to offload those units so you make a profit.

- There is also risk. E.g. imagine if you're bid order is filled at 98 and you have an ask at 100, but then the price drops to
95. Your ask never gets filled, so you never make a profit from the buy you made at 98.

- Inventory tracking is hence very important for market makers, you need to ensure that you cancel/react accordingly before the
market moves away from you.

- Tightness of spread is also important. If the spread is too wide, you might have offers that are too unrealistic, 
decreasing the likelihood of fills and reducing your volume. If you're spread is too tight, you'll
end up with lower profits. 

- With tight spreads, you're also more exposed to well informed traders as you're closer to fair price. E.g. you might have placed
a bid at 99 and an ask at 101. A well informed trader might be aware of a price decrease in the future, so sells to your good bid. You're left with the ask that loses out to a price decrease. If your spread was very wide, you'd be far away from fair on either side, so there's no reason for a well informed trader to trade them.
